Reflective Assignment: My Clinical Placement on a Surgical Ward

For this reflection I will use Gibbs' reflective cycle to discuss an experience from my second-year placement on a general surgical ward, where I was involved in caring for an elderly patient recovering from a hip replacement.

Description
During a morning shift, I was assigned to help care for Mrs H., an 82-year-old woman who was two days post-operation. While assisting her to mobilise with a frame, I noticed she seemed more confused than the day before and was unsure where she was. I mentioned this to my mentor, who assessed her and suspected delirium, possibly caused by a urinary tract infection.

Feelings
At first I felt uncertain about whether my observation was important enough to report, as I did not want to waste my mentor's time. Afterwards I felt relieved that I had spoken up, and also slightly anxious that I might have missed other signs earlier in the shift.

Evaluation
The positive aspect of this experience was that early reporting led to a prompt assessment and treatment plan. However, I realised that I did not have a clear understanding of the difference between dementia and delirium, which made me hesitant.

Analysis
Delirium is common in older surgical patients and is associated with longer hospital stays and worse outcomes, so early recognition is essential. The NICE guidelines emphasise that any sudden change in cognition or behaviour should be treated as possible delirium. My hesitation reflects a common issue among students, who may lack confidence in escalating concerns.

Conclusion
This experience showed me that my observations as a student can make a real difference to patient care, and that it is always better to report a change than to stay silent.

Action plan
In future placements I will complete the trust's online delirium training, use a structured tool such as the 4AT when I notice confusion, and practise using the SBAR format so that I can communicate concerns clearly and confidently.
